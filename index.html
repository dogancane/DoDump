Validation and diagnostics

Is this physics or numerics?

Treat a result as possibly meaningful only if: the count and mass balances close, maximum overlap stays below about 5 % of radius, no particles escape through walls, and the same outcome appears with a different seed, a halved time step and stiffer contacts. Use the buttons to rerun with one change at a time and compare in Results.

Validation benchmarks (2D experiments)

These tests run this exact engine on published 2D clogging experiments with identical dry disks, 12 seeds per case. Hopper: To et al. (2001), V-funnel, about 200 disks; jams always at opening/disk = 2 and never at 5. Vertical pipe, packed column released: Zhou et al. (2025), friction 0.4; stable three-disk arches for D/d between about 2.45 and 3, flow outside that band. Passing them shows the 2D contact and arching physics is right; it does not make 2D results valid for a round 3D pipe (Janda et al. 2015: clogging up to D/d50 of about 6.8).

Stone generation

  1. A stone mass M is drawn from the grade (measured masses or a curve inside the standard envelope).
  2. Nominal diameter Dn = (M / rho)1/3. This is the equivalent cube size, not a measured length.
  3. Shape class (default model, V2.4) drawn from the grade's class mix after Zingg: blocky/cubic (B/L and T/B 0.67-1), long and thin (B/L 0.40-0.67), flat/slab (T/B 0.40-0.67), bladed (both 0.40-0.67) and rounded/near circular (both 0.75-1). Axis ratios are uniform inside the class limits. Measured L/B/T records of the grade are used first where they exist. Default mix 55/15/15/10/5 % is an ASSUMPTION guided by Domokos et al. (2020: natural fragments are cube-like on average) and the Rock Manual (L/T > 3 typically < 5 % for heavy and < 20 % for light gradings). Replace it with measured shape counts of the actual rock.
  4. Block size from the volume factor: V = vf L B T = Dn3, vf 0.6 for blocks (ASSUMPTION, cuboid 1.0, ellipsoid 0.524) and pi/6 for rounded stones.
  5. Legacy L/T model (selectable): elongation L/T from a truncated normal, intermediate axis B = T (L/T)phi.
  6. Ellipsoid L x B x T with the same volume: (pi/6) L B T = Dn3. Optionally L is capped at (Lmax/Dn) x Dn from the grade table.
  7. Orientation: a uniform random 3D rotation, then the shadow of the block (8 corners) or ellipsoid on the section plane gives the outline (or the worst case: long axis in plane).
  8. Blocks: the shadow rectangle is filled with a row of core circles plus four small corner circles (radius 0.3 of the half thickness by default), giving flat faces and corners that lock against walls and other stones. Clumps of spheres still round off sharp edges (Suhr & Six 2020), so very angular contact is approximated, not reproduced. The view draws the outline as the hull of the contact circles; tick "Contact spheres" to see what the DEM uses.
  9. Ellipsoids (rounded class and legacy model): the outline is filled with up to 7 overlapping circles. The interior circles are inscribed in the ellipse and the end circles have the tip curvature b2/a. An irregularity factor perturbs the radii and offsets to approximate angular stones. Stones rotate freely and interlock through these clumps.

Inertia is m (a2 + b2)/5 using the projected semi-axes. Drag uses the mean projected area of the 3D ellipsoid (surface area / 4, independent of orientation) with an input Cd.

Contact physics

  • Normal: linear spring-dashpot, Fn = k delta + c vn, no tension, after Cundall & Strack (1979). The damping coefficient follows from the restitution coefficient: c = 2 zeta sqrt(k m), zeta = -ln e / sqrt(pi^2 + ln^2 e).
  • Tangential: incremental spring capped by Coulomb friction |Ft| <= mu Fn. Stored static friction is what lets arches stand.
  • Rolling: constant directional torque muR Reff Fn, one of the model families compared by Ai et al. (2011).
  • Stiffness scales with the stones' nominal radii, k = kn Reff / Reff(Dn50). kn is set so a reference stone (M90 by default) hitting at vChar overlaps by the target percentage of Dn50. Contacts are deliberately softer than real rock (standard DEM practice).
  • Walls: friction, restitution and rolling resistance come from the bucket material. Stiffness is multiplied by the Hertz effective-modulus ratio E*(rock-wall) / E*(rock-rock), where 1/E* = (1 - nu1^2)/E1 + (1 - nu2^2)/E2. The ratio is clipped to the set limits, because PE walls are about 20 times softer than rock and would give unrealistic overlaps in a softened model.
  • Time step: a fraction of the shortest contact duration pi sqrt(m_eff / k), for both stone-stone and stone-wall contacts.

Pipe tilt, current and FP ROV (V2.7)

The bucket string can be vertical, tilted at one angle, given a top angle plus a change per joint, or shaped by current with the FP ROV holding the tip.

  • Shape in current (static chain). Each bucket is a rigid link from the previous lip to its own lip; the string bends only at the joints. Equilibrium of everything below a point gives tan theta = H / T, with T = W_b + sum w L (ROV or bottom weight in water, buckets in water, chains; links above the water level weigh in air) and H = H_b + sum f L. Current drag per metre is the Morison drag term f = 1/2 rho Cd D_out U_rel |U_rel|, with U_rel = U(z) - V_vessel and U(z) = U0 or U0 ((d - z)/d)^(1/7).
  • FP ROV. Free tip: H_b is the drag on the ROV. ROV holding position: H_b is solved so the tip offset equals the target; the ROV thrust needed is H_b - F_ROV, compared with its maximum. Above the maximum the thrust is capped and the tip drifts. For a light string and uniform current the ROV needs about half the string drag plus its own drag, and the string bows with top and bottom angles of about +/- f L / (2 W_b).
  • Checks. Top angle against the allowed angle in the moonpool (design dependent; 7 deg while dumping in patent US8628287), each joint angle against its geometric limit tan = 2 c / ins, string tension (buoyant HDPE buckets hang only from the ROV and chains).
  • Effect on the rock. The DEM pipe stays straight and gravity is turned by the local link angle: g_pipe = (-g' sin theta, -g' cos theta). Stones drift to the lower wall at about v_t sqrt(sin theta) and slide along it; a sliding stone is driven by g' (cos theta - mu sin theta). Wall friction, rolling and impacts at the joints come from the contact model. Curvature terms v^2 / R are neglected. The tilt lies in the simulated plane (worst case).
  • Not modelled: vessel motion and dynamic response, vortex-induced vibration, current direction out of the simulated plane, cross-flow reduction of drag on inclined links (conservative for small angles), loads from the rock inside the string, bending stiffness of a continuous pipe.

Water effect (scour-protection dumping through the moonpool)

The fall pipe hangs through the moonpool and is open to the sea at the bottom. Its top is above the water surface in the moonpool (input "Pipe top above water", default 6 m). In the coupled water model:

  • Dry part in the moonpool. Above the water surface the stones fall in air: full weight, no buoyancy, no added mass, air drag only (rho 1.2 kg/m3). They reach the water at v = sqrt(v0^2 + 2 g H), about 11 m/s after 6 m, well above their terminal velocity in water, and are braked by drag below the surface.
  • Free surface inside the pipe. No water can enter through the top, so the inside water surface is free (atmospheric) and is drawn down below the moonpool level when the rock pulls water downward: p' = -rho g z at the surface, dz/dt = Q_top / A. Through-flow is then fed only by the leaky joints. With sealed joints there is no steady flow, and the level drops until rho g z A carries the rock drag on the water column. Joints above the moonpool water level exchange air and are excluded. Setting the input to 0 gives a submerged inlet with entrance loss K_in.
  • Entrained flow. Falling stones hand their submerged weight to the water through drag. This pulls a downward water flow through the pipe, supplied by the joints (or the top inlet when it is submerged): the induced flow described by Chen et al. 2015. The water is modelled as a 1D column along the whole wet string, not only the DEM window. Rock loading: the drag reaction measured in the wet part of the DEM window, plus the steady settling load (rho_s - rho_w)/rho_s g mdot / v_s on the wet string outside the window (v_s from Richardson-Zaki with the measured rock inflow), plus the momentum flux mdot (v_entry - v_s) at the water surface when the surface lies outside the window.
  • Losses that limit the flow:
    • Entrance loss K_in at the top inlet, plus the velocity head.
    • Darcy wall friction f L/D. f comes from Haaland: 1/sqrt(f) = -1.8 log10[(e/3.7D)^1.11 + 6.9/Re], using the material roughness.
    • A Borda-Carnot sudden-expansion loss rho (u1 - u2)^2 / 2 wherever a bucket lip discharges into the wider bucket below.
    • Lossless Bernoulli changes along each cone.
    • A free jet at the outlet; reverse flow into the outlet takes an entrance loss.
  • Leaky joints. Each transition gap (the annular pocket, area = mean diameter x pi x radial clearance) is an orifice to the sea, q = Cd A sqrt(2|p'|/rho). Water flows in where the inside pressure is below ambient (upper part) and out where it is above. Buckets between gaps form reaches with their own water inertia rho sum L/A, integrated semi-implicitly. The water starts at rest, so the start-up is included.
  • Effects on the stones:
    • Drag acts on the slip velocity relative to the local interstitial water velocity Q/(A eps).
    • Dense flow increases drag through the Di Felice (1994) voidage function eps^(2 - chi), with chi = 3.7 - 0.65 exp(-(1.5 - log10 Re)^2/2). Local voidage comes from the stones in the slice: 3D solid fraction = 2/3 of the in-plane area fraction (assumption).
    • The pressure gradient of the moving water acts on each stone (-V dp'/ds).
    • Stones carry an added mass Ca x displaced water mass (0.5 for a sphere).
    • The drag reaction on the water closes the two-way coupling.
  • Check: an analytical sealed-pipe momentum balance (submerged rock weight per metre against all losses, slip from Richardson-Zaki) is shown next to the DEM-coupled value.
  • Not modelled: velocity profile across the pipe, turbulence and wakes, clustering, air carried in at the moonpool, the jet and spreading at the seabed, current, vessel motion. These need CFD-DEM (level 3). The still-water option reproduces V2 for comparison.

Verification and validation status (V2.2)

  • Defect found and fixed. Up to V2.1 the feed placed one stone at a time at the top of the chute, so high requested rates were never delivered. For example, only about 25 % of 25 000 t/h actually entered, and this was reported only as YELLOW. Stones are now released anywhere in a release zone with a shape-level overlap test. The share of the requested feed that the inlet accepts is measured and reported. If the inlet accepts less than the blocked ratio for the persistence time, it is classed as an inlet choke (RED).
  • 2D physics validated against 2D experiments (Diagnostics, Run benchmarks):
    • 2D hopper (To et al. 2001): jams always at opening/disk = 2 and never at 5. Engine: 9/10 at 2, 7/10 at 3, 0/10 at 4 to 6.
    • 2D vertical pipe with a packed column (Zhou et al. 2025, friction 0.4): clogging only between about 2.45 and 3. Engine: 0/12 at 2.2, 7/12 at 2.6, 0/12 at 3.0 to 4.0.
  • Not valid for 3D round-pipe probabilities. In 3D narrow vertical pipes, dense flow clogs up to D/d50 ≈ 6.8 or D/d95 ≈ 4.7 (Janda et al. 2015). A 2D section only forms three-stone arches, so inside that range the DEM under-predicts blockage. The Checks panel flags this.
  • Flow regime matters. Faster discharge and kinetic energy break incipient arches (Janda et al. 2015; Arévalo & Zuriguel 2016). Water makes clogging only slightly more likely in hoppers (Koivisto & Durian 2017). A dilute, fast fall-pipe stream is therefore far less clog-prone than a dense one at the same size ratio. The dense cases are the critical ones:
    • overfeeding
    • a full hopper or funnel at the inlet (feed mode "gravity-fed full hopper", funnel option)
    • restart with rock left in the pipe (Restart option: window filled, settled on a closed outlet gate, then released)
    • accumulation at ledges
  • Industry context: fall pipe feeding up to about 2000 t/h (Chen et al. 2015); rock up to about 400 mm (IADC); DEME Flintstone fall pipe 650 mm. LMA 60/300 (Dn up to about 554 mm) is above typical fall-pipe rock sizes.

Continuous mass-controlled flow

The feed releases a stone whenever the released mass is below Q_slice x t, at a random lateral position inside the feed opening, with the set inlet velocity. Placement waits if the chute entry is occupied, and that time is counted. Stones that pass the exit zone or leave the domain release their slot to a free list after a two-step quarantine, so stale friction history is never reused. Body capacity is sized from the window area. If it fills, the feed waits and diagnostics report it. Count and mass balances are checked continuously.

Slice scaling. A 3D mass flow Q is converted to the simulated plane by the ratio of a Dn50-thick strip to the pipe area. All discharged masses are converted back to 3D-equivalent tonnes the same way. This sets the stone concentration and is a modelling assumption.

Grade data and the batch test

  • Rule: values are entered as given. Missing values stay "not given". Derived values are marked and show their formula.
  • Sources:
    • T1: your LMA tables (limit masses, Dn, maximum expected length, Ds).
    • T2: your class table (M50 range for category A, D50 lower/upper, D85/D15, Dn50).
    • S1: prEN 13383-1:2021 (CEN enquiry draft). Gives the CP 32/90 sieve envelope, the LMA/LMB mass limits for grades not in T1, and the confirmation that LMB has no Mem requirement.
    • B1: your batch test.
  • Derived from your data:
    • rho = 2650 kg/m³ reproduces every Dn in T1.
    • Lmax/Dn = 1.80 at every limit.
    • T2 Dn50/D50(mid) = 0.82 to 0.86, so the class-table D50 is a sieve-type size.
  • Envelope curves are % lighter by mass.
    • Light gradings are interpolated with a Rosin-Rammler curve y = 1 - exp(ln 0.5 (M/M50)^n) through the NLL and NUL points (the CIRIA/CUR/CETMEF 2007 interpolation). Log-linear interpolation is available as an alternative.
    • The position in the envelope is selectable: fine edge, mid, coarse edge, or the curve whose number-mean matches the Mem midpoint.
    • Tails beyond ELL and EUL are truncated and the truncated mass share is reported.
    • Mass fractions are converted to number weights by dividing each mass increment by its stone mass. Skipping this would heavily over-represent large stones.
  • Measured masses (B1 or an import) are sampled directly by bootstrap. This reproduces the measured number distribution exactly, and the mass distribution follows. Optional log-space kernel smoothing is clipped to the measured range. Fragments weighed in bulk cannot be sampled as individual stones and are excluded (reported).
  • Validation of imports:
    • Missing or non-numeric and zero or negative masses inside the data block.
    • Tukey fences on log mass (1.5 and 3 IQR).
    • Out-of-order rows and repeated values.
    • Conformity with the grade limits by mass (fragments counted).
    • Mem.
    • Number- and mass-weighted percentiles and histograms.
    If L, B and T columns are given they are stored with the grade. Otherwise only the equivalent Dn is available and is labelled as such.

Blockage detection (per run)

  • Inlet and outlet mass are counted where stones cross s = 0 and s = L of the DEM window. Rates use a moving window that is extended automatically to hold about 10 stones at the nominal rate, because sparse coarse-rock flow is noisy.
  • Axial bins: count, in-plane solid fraction and mean axial velocity. A bin is a dense stalled zone if its solid fraction is above the threshold and its mean axial speed is below the stall speed.
  • Stalled stones are linked through the actual contact list. A connected group touching both walls is a spanning bridge. A single stone touching both walls is a single-stone obstruction.
  • Bridges are tracked over time. A bridge that disappears for longer than the break tolerance has broken (temporary). A bridge older than the persistence time while outlet flow is at or below the blocked ratio is a persistent blockage. Outflow stopping with stalled stones in the window for at least max(persistence time, 5 stone intervals) is also persistent.
  • Run classes: free, restricted (YELLOW or worse for more than the set share of the assessed time), temporary bridging, persistent blockage.

Blockage probability

  • N runs with different seeds per configuration. Each run gets a class. The probability of each class and of "restricted or worse" and "bridging or worse" is k/n with a 95 % Wilson score interval.
  • Persistent blockage is also treated as a Poisson process in discharged mass, after Thomas & Durian (2015), who show clogging is Poissonian. Runs that blocked contribute the mass discharged before blockage; runs that did not block contribute their full discharged mass as censored exposure.
    • Rate: lambda = r / M.
    • 95 % bounds: chi2(0.025; 2r) / 2M and chi2(0.975; 2r+2) / 2M.
    • Probability of blockage within mass X: P(X) = 1 - exp(-lambda X).
  • For each blocking run the report gives the time and mass discharged before blockage, the depth and nearest feature, and the Dn and L of the stones in the arch. The assessment never uses only D50 or a single ratio.

Analytical and empirical checks

  • Analytical Sieve principle: a stone whose intermediate axis B exceeds a round opening cannot pass in any orientation. The tool reports the share of generated stones with B >= clear passage, and with L >= clear passage (these stones can span when misaligned).
  • Analytical Terminal velocity vt = sqrt(4/3 g Dn (rho_s - rho_w) / (rho_w Cd)).
  • Empirical Hindered settling, Richardson-Zaki v = vt (1 - Cv)^beta, with beta = 2.39 for coarse particles (Miedema). The maximum solids flux through the narrowest passage is reached at Cv = 1/(1 + beta). A demand above it means dense flow is required. Water entrained downward by the rock raises the real capacity.
  • Empirical Thomas & Durian (2015): fraction of clogging configurations F = exp[-C (x^(alpha/2) - 1)], with x = (D/d)^2, C = 0.14 and alpha = 3 in 3D. Mean mass before clog <m> = rho_b A (0.75 d) / F. Dry monodisperse spheres in hoppers.
  • Empirical Zuriguel et al. (2005): no jams above opening/grain diameter of about 4.9 for spheres and about 6.2 for rice-shaped grains (3D silo). Janda et al. (2008): in 2D the jamming probability decays but no true critical size exists. Börzsönyi et al. (2016): elongated grains form different clogging domes.
  • Empirical Beverloo: W = C rho_b sqrt(g) (D - k d)^(5/2), C about 0.58, k material dependent. This is for dry discharge only and is shown as an order of magnitude.

Bucket string, transitions and materials

  • Data: string → 1 to 30 stacks → 1 to 19 buckets each (stack count and buckets per stack are independent, with per-stack overrides). A bucket type holds top/bottom ID, length, wall thickness, insertion length and material. Each bucket can override any field. Stack joints can have their own insertion.
  • Geometry: sTop(i+1) = sBot(i) - ins(i).
    • Radial clearance at the upper lip: (ID(i+1) at the insertion depth - OD(i) at the bottom) / 2. Zero or negative means a clash and blocks the run.
    • The clear flow path through each transition is the smallest internal diameter over the overlap zone.
    • The annular pocket (width = clearance, depth = insertion) and the lip step are drawn as solid wall faces, so stones can lodge in them.
  • DEM window: a selectable run of consecutive buckets, centred by default on the narrowest transition. The whole string (up to 570 buckets) is too long for a browser DEM, so the inlet of the window takes either the input velocity or the terminal velocity of Dn50.
  • Structure, per bucket (thin conical shell):
    • Volume V.
    • Mass rho_mat V and submerged weight (rho_mat - rho_w) g V. HDPE is buoyant, so this is negative.
    • EA and EI at mid-height.
    • Allowable axial load sAllow x A, with no safety factor.
    These feed the future catenary module.
  • Materials: PE (generic), HDPE (PE100), MDPE (PE80), steel S355, plus custom materials. Every property carries a source tag (datasheet, standard, by definition, typical, assumption). PE stiffness is short-term; creep is not modelled.

Future coupled system

The modules are separate files with defined interfaces. In V2, catenary/dynamics, hydrodynamics (Morison-type drag and added mass with current profile), vessel offset and 6-DOF motion, and the Fall Pipe ROV (free body, position control, or fixed point only if deliberately selected) only store their inputs. Planned coupling per time step:

  1. Vessel state.
  2. Hydrodynamic loads.
  3. ROV loads.
  4. Lumped-mass catenary step.
  5. Bucket offsets and tilts fed back to the DEM geometry.
  6. Internal rock mass and wall loads fed back to the catenary.

The rock-flow module is to be validated before any of these couplings are switched on.

Major assumptions and limitations

  • Planar section. Stones move in one plane. Arches in 2D and domes in 3D need different numbers of stones, so thresholds found here do not transfer directly to a round pipe without calibration.
  • Water is 1D. Entrained flow, joint leakage, hindrance, pressure gradient and added mass are included as a 1D column model. The velocity profile, turbulence, wakes, clustering, air entrainment and the seabed jet are not; real pressure transients (Chen et al. 2015) need CFD.
  • Shape. Where no L/B/T data exist, the shape class mix is an assumption. The batch test has only 5 shape records. Contacts remain rounded, and there is no breakage or abrasion.
  • Soft contacts with a clipped material stiffness ratio. Check sensitivity with the Diagnostics buttons.
  • Grade curves. Spec-envelope curves are synthetic; only measured masses represent a real batch. Fragments below ELL are not sampled.
  • Rigid coaxial string. No catenary, misalignment, vessel motion or ROV in V2.
  • Statistics. Probabilities are conditional on the model. They describe how often this model blocks, not how often a real fall pipe will.
